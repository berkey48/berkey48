**Sport Scientist | Performance Analyst** · M.S. Kinesiology (Data Science Track), Seattle University · CPSS, CSCS · Seattle, WA

I turn athlete-monitoring data into decisions coaches can act on: training load, readiness, and return-to-play. My foundation is hands-on. ## Featured projects

### [NCAA Collegiate Soccer Performance Report](https://github.com/berkey48/NCAA-Collegiate-Soccer-Report)
*M.S. Capstone, Seattle University*

A multi-format coaching report for an inaugural PlayerData GPS season. It tracks training load and match-day intensity build-up across a full season and was built around what the coaching staff needed to see.

- Reactive **Shiny** dashboard, static **Crosstalk** HTML, and a PDF season report
- Metric selection and load-periodization recommendations aligned with coach needs


### [SQL and ETL Samples](https://github.com/berkey48/SQL-and-ETL-Samples)
*Coursework*

SQL queries and ETL examples from my data science coursework, in Jupyter Notebook.

### Athlete Monitoring SOP
*Seattle University, Athletic Performance Department*

Designed and rolled out standard operating procedures for GPS-based athlete monitoring across multiple collegiate teams, covering device setup, session management, data-quality workflows, and graduate assistant onboarding. Source documents are internal, so a sanitized template is on the way.

### Integrating Technology in Baseball: A Multi-System Swing Analytics Framework
*Seattle University, Technology in Performance Science*

Assessed the validated technology landscape for baseball swing kinematics (IMU, AI-assisted markerless motion capture, and radar) and proposed a data-fusion framework that uses acoustic-triggered time alignment to combine all three into one pipeline. Includes a phased implementation roadmap and a stakeholder analysis across players, coaches, and administration.

### Barbell Squat Velocity as a Measure of an Athlete's Readiness
*Seattle University Student Research & Creativity Conference, May 2025*

A research framework investigating velocity-based training (VBT) as a readiness indicator for Division I athletes, exploring the relationship between mean concentric velocity and session RPE to quantify neuromuscular fatigue without subjective bias.
